I personally noticed that modal windows are everywhere, ironically when the moment I opened this article a pop-up took up the window asking to subscribe to the author’s work.

From my experience, the “escape hatch” in modals that want the user to interact with something are usually difficult to locate, which could be designed intentionally. Highlighted buttons are nonexistent, and instead a small ‘x’ hides in a corner. Another feature that occurs often are sudden pop-ups. Most users want to have control over navigating the website for their own purposes.

The discussion of mobile modals stood out to me. In Interactive Media I, I learned that web traffic is increasing on mobile devices in comparison to desktop. Designing modals on mobile view seems challenging because the phone screen is significantly narrower and can’t fit too much content. When a modal takes up the screen, it completely obstructs the background which I find troublesome. I’m curious about other alternatives for modals on mobile view.

Research Form Design

Article

This article made me reflect on the effectiveness of a designer's considerations for certain elements in a form. I believe the "less is more" theory is the main takeaway to prioritize the user experience. A form should be simple and quick to compelete, guiding the user through each step. There may be blind spots that designers may not cover which should be addressed, for example, I did not know some users use "tab" to move to the next field. I noticed that many accessibility features don't exist on most forms, especially changing text size which I didn't know could be a feature.

An Effective Form

A form that best exemplifies these practices is the Duolingo onboarding process. The text options are accompanied by icons and surrounded in individual buttons. Every option selected responds with confirmed feedback. The overall onboarding process is not too long, taking around a minute to complete which is efficient for the user.
